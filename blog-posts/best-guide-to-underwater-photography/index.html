Best Guide to Underwater Photography

From the Devocean dive journal.

October 7, 2025

Capturing the Depths: A Guide to Scuba and Cave Diving Photography

Welcome to the world beneath the earth and sea — where light fades, silence reigns, and every shot tells a story few ever see.

Scuba and cave diving photography isn’t just about taking pictures underwater; it’s about mastering your environment, your buoyancy, and your gear to capture moments hidden in the dark. Whether you’re transitioning from open-water photography or just starting to explore the magic of the underworld, this guide will help you take your underwater imagery to the next level.

Choosing the Right Gear

Before submerging into the depths, your equipment becomes your lifeline and your creative toolkit.

Invest in a reliable underwater camera housing rated for your maximum depth. For cave photography, choose housings with sturdy handles and secure seals to withstand tight spaces and silt-heavy environments.

You’ll also need:

Powerful lights and strobes: Darkness is absolute in caves — lighting is everything. Carry at least two lights for redundancy.

Wide-angle or fisheye lenses: Perfect for capturing sweeping passages or large scenes in limited visibility.

Secure mounting and tether systems: To prevent loss or damage in confined spaces.

Before you dive, test your setup in shallow water. Check for leaks, balance your buoyancy with the camera rig, and practice shooting while maintaining trim and control.

Mastering Buoyancy and Movement

In cave photography, control is your art form. Even a small fin kick can stir up silt, destroying visibility and ruining a perfect shot.

Practice neutral buoyancy and frog kicks to minimize disturbance. Keep your movements slow and deliberate — you’re painting with light in total darkness.

Equalize early and often, maintain awareness of your surroundings, and never push past your training limits just to “get the shot.” The best photo is the one you come home with safely.

Lighting the Darkness

Light behaves differently underground. You’ll learn to work with backscatter, diffused reflections, and shadows that tell stories.

Experiment with off-camera lighting to highlight formations or silhouettes of divers. Use wide, even light for large chambers, and focused beams for dramatic effects in narrow tunnels.

Remember — in cave diving, less is often more. Subtle lighting reveals the mystery; harsh light kills it.

Respect the Environment

Every cave and reef is a fragile ecosystem shaped over millennia. Your bubbles, fins, and lights can alter it in seconds.

Never touch formations or wildlife.

Keep a safe distance from walls and sediments.

Avoid over-lighting sensitive creatures.

A true underwater photographer leaves no trace — only images that inspire others to protect these hidden worlds.

Planning Your Dives

Each photo dive starts on land.

Plan your gas, light, and time limits with precision. Discuss roles with your dive buddy or model — communication and coordination are key in overhead environments.

Scout your site in advance, understand the cave’s layout, and always respect access rules. When in doubt, choose safety over the shot. The caves will still be there tomorrow.

The Reward Beneath the Surface

Scuba and cave photography is equal parts art, exploration, and discipline. It teaches patience, focus, and deep respect for the unknown.

When the beam of your light cuts through darkness to reveal an untouched chamber — and your camera captures that stillness — you’re not just taking a photo. You’re documenting a world few will ever see.

So, gear up. Light up the dark. And bring back stories carved in stone and silence.
